Ubunture UTokyo

Youth are Our Future

A student-led international collaboration project from the University of Tokyo. University of Tokyo and South African university students work together as equal partners, tackling South Africa's education gap by developing and running ICT education programs that expand what's possible for children. Together, we also build exchange between students of both countries, strengthening the bilateral relationship through the next generation.

About

About Us

Mission: Youth are Our Future

Ubunture UTokyo is a student organization from the University of Tokyo that expands children's freedom to learn and their power to dream through digital education. University of Tokyo and South African university students collaborate as equal partners to develop and run digital education programs, opening up the future for children and students alike.

Activities

Our Activities

From the challenges we see on the ground, to the activities we run, to the value it creates — here's how our work fits together.

01 · CHALLENGES

The Challenges

South Africa's education and digital-skills gap, and the need to strengthen ties between Japan and South Africa to help close it.

02 · ACTIVITIES

Our Activities

Running lessons and developing teaching materials directly with children and teachers, while building lasting partnerships with South African universities and student organizations.

03 · VALUE

The Value We Create

Solving local challenges, developing the university students involved, and strengthening ties between Japan and South Africa.

Two Pillars: Education and Partnership

We work across two pillars: education — running pilot lessons and developing teaching materials directly with children and teachers at local schools — and creating partnerships with local universities and student organizations, including the University of Pretoria, the University of Cape Town, and Stellenbosch University. Alongside that, we work to strengthen ties between Japan and South Africa by having students from both countries learn together as equal partners.

Interested in digital skills as well, works on robotics and math-driven problem solving using digital technology, and has achieved results in competitions in Japan and abroad—bridging science & technology with education.

Aria Mitani

2nd year, College of Arts & Sciences (Humanities I), The University of Tokyo

Has collaborated with students from Japan and abroad through a field program in the Philippines focusing on urban and agricultural development and poverty, and by organizing student conferences in Boston and Tokyo. Active in Ubunture UTokyo since its launch last year, exploring approaches to educational cooperation through fundraising, curriculum design, and field visits. Aims to promote international educational cooperation that expands children's possibilities by connecting people across countries and backgrounds.
