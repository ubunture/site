About

About Us

Ubunture UTokyo is a student organization originating from the University of Tokyo that aims to expand children's "freedom to learn" and "power to dream" globally through digital education. Built on the philosophy of Ubuntu ("I am because you are") combined with Future, our name reflects our commitment to bringing diverse people together to co-create meaningful learning experiences for social impact.

We collaborate with local partners to deliver unplugged digital skills education in Johannesburg, South Africa.

Why

Why Unplugged Digital Education?

In South Africa, digital education disparities exist in a double structure: disparities in device access and disparities in skills application. A double barrier of connectivity and classroom application (low internet adoption at home and lack of teacher training in digital skills) leaves many children behind.

To address this, we introduce "Unplugged Digital Skills Education" which teaches the fundamentals of computational thinking—such as decomposition, conditional logic, repetition, and verification—without needing computers or electricity, paired with teacher training workshops.

Activities

Activities (Students / Teachers)

Student Activities (Direct Support)

  • Target: Upper elementary to middle school students in under-resourced schools
  • Designing 4 main modules (with 2 variations each) tailored by grade levels
  • Materials: Worksheets, instruction cards, abacuses, household items, simplified boards
  • Goal: Hands-on experiences → Building the foundation of logical thinking

Teacher Activities (ToT: Training of Trainers)

  • 2-Part Structure: ① Unplugged classroom workshop / ② Cross-curricular integration
  • Observation & hands-on experience of student activities + lesson design practice
  • Assessment, material management, and year-round planning for sustainable usage
  • Goal: Ensuring continuation and self-operation without Japanese staff present
Curriculum

Curriculum Details

Module A: Abacus & Algorithms

  • Content: Decomposition / Procedural games (conditionals)
  • Target: Grades 5–7 | 45–60 min × 2 sessions
  • Materials: Abacuses, worksheets, instruction cards
  • Outcomes: Formulating calculation steps and understanding iteration

Module B: Storybook Coding

  • Content: Extracting steps from stories → Writing pseudocode
  • Target: Grades 4–6 | 45 min
  • Materials: Story sheets, pseudocode cards
  • Outcomes: Abstraction, structural thinking, and collaborative learning

Module C: Offline Logic Apps

  • Content: Conditional games using offline smartphone apps
  • Target: Grades 7–9 | 45–60 min
  • Materials: Smartphones (shared), paper-based step cards
  • Outcomes: Applying logic to real-world scenarios

*Curriculum will be adjusted and translated to meet local needs.

Evaluation

Evaluation Design

  • Pre/Post Tests (measuring changes in logical thinking and learning motivation)
  • Classroom observation rubrics (participation, collaboration, problem-solving processes)
  • Teacher surveys (feasibility of adoption, motivation for continuity, areas of improvement)
  • Findings will be published in a Summary Report (A4, ~20 pages) and a Detailed Report (~50 pages)

Risks

Risks & Mitigation

Operations

  • Visa & safety: Complying with local partner guidelines and pre-approving transport routes
  • Power outages & network issues: Unplugged design ensures no disruption to lessons
  • School closures or strikes: Coordinating backup dates and locations in advance

Financials & Governance

  • Receipt account is currently under the representative's personal name: Balances are audited by the group, with annual public reports
  • Issuing receipts and invoices under the organization to ensure transparency
  • Expenses managed with authorization checks and documentation, ensuring accountability
FAQ

FAQ

Q. What is Unplugged Digital Education?

A. It is a learning method that teaches computational logic, steps, and conditionals using cards, paper, and physical objects instead of PCs and power outlets. We bridge to digital once these basics are set.

Q. Will results be shared publicly?

A. Yes. Pre/post test outcomes and classroom observation notes will be compiled and published in a Summary Report (20p) and Detailed Report (50p).

Q. Can local teachers continue on their own?

A. Yes. The handouts, lesson plans, and rubrics are designed for local teachers to sustain the program independently.
